# SO-101 (follower): The Robot Studio's STEP to a MuJoCo description

`src/so101.py` reads `upstream/so101/STEP/SO101/SO101 Assembly.step` and returns the articulated arm.
Its `__main__` builds the arm and writes the plain exports, `out/so101/export/so101.xml` and
`so101.urdf`, with their meshes (`assets/` for the MJCF, `meshes/` for the URDF): each part collides
as its mesh, and there are no actuators. `src/so101_mujoco.py` is the arm's MuJoCo function: it makes
the model a `mujoco.MjSpec`, chooses the collisions, adds the servos' actuators and a commanded pose,
and saves `out/so101/so101.xml`, the file the comparison with Menagerie reads. The geometry helpers are in
`src/lib/step_links.py`, and the joint-stop sweep is in `src/so101_ranges.py`. Every number in the
model comes from three sources: the STEP's geometry, the STEP's materials, or a physical source named
beside the number. Menagerie's `robotstudio_so101` and the maker's `so101_new_calib.urdf` were read
for three things only: names, which printed parts make which link, and, after the model was built,
the comparison.

```
cd models/mujoco
python src/so101.py          # build + export (daemon), then:
python src/so101_ranges.py   # the joint stops, from the exported MJCF
python src/compare.py so101  # vs Menagerie and the maker's URDF
```

## The STEP

The file is a Fusion 360 export, 20 MB, with 97 occurrences under `SO101 Assembly v4`. It holds:

- **11 printed parts.** These are base, `Base_08p` (the base motor holder), the WaveShare mounting
  plate, the rotation-pitch bracket, the base motor holder, the upper arm, the under arm, the wrist
  motor holder, the wrist roll pitch, the wrist roll follower (the static jaw and the gripper servo's
  cradle in one print), and the moving jaw.
  - The top-level printed solids carry "ABS Plastic" (1060 kg/m^3).
  - The printed bodies nested in a component (`Upper_arm_SO101 v9:1/SOLID` and the like) read with
    **no material**.
  - The WaveShare plate is "Steel": Fusion's default, not the real material.
- **Six `ST3215 Servo v2:N`.** Each is a `SCS215 v6_7` sub-assembly of 8 leaves:
  - three case shells: `ZK_122` (middle), `SG-ZIJI_15` and `XG-ZIJI_16`;
  - `MOTOR-1723_3`, `PCB-CHAZUO_92` and `GE_27` (gears), all "PPS 塑料" at 1637 kg/m^3;
  - two steel horns ("钢", 7850 kg/m^3): `金属舵盘（驱动）`, the drive horn on the spline, and
    `金属舵盘（从动）`, the idler.
  - Servo 5 (the wrist roll) has no idler horn.
- **71 fasteners on the arm** at their own steels:
  - 24 ANSI fillister tapping screws (Steel Grade 2), 22 of #1 and 2 of #0, 7.9-8.0 g/cm^3;
  - 45 M3x6 pan head screws (Steel 4.6);
  - 2 M3 nuts (Steel 6);
  - 4 M2.5 screws and 4 hex spacers more, which belong to the board.
- **Two Molex `050375033` connector housings**, with no material.
- **`Bus Servo Adapter (A) v3`**, the driver board: 46 children and about 120 leaves, every one "Steel".
- **`Component1..7`**, six swept servo cables (3.85 cm^3 in all), "Steel".
- 11 empty `Wiring_holder` components.

**Pose.** The STEP is in the SO-101's folded rest pose:

- The base stands on z = 0.4.
- The upper arm lies level, pointing back (+Y) from the shoulder at (y -39, z 120) to the elbow at
  (y 74, z 148).
- The forearm lies on top of it, pointing forward (-Y) to the wrist flex at (y -61, z 152).
- The wrist is straight. The gripper points forward along -Y, and its static jaw tip is at y = -226.
- The moving jaw is **open by about 90 degrees**: it stands straight up, z 162-254.

That pose is every joint's zero here. In the references' coordinates, our zero is at shoulder_lift
-89.3, elbow_flex +89.9 and gripper +87.6 degrees, with every other joint within 0.3 degrees, apart
from wrist_roll at about -13 degrees (an ambiguous match, below).

**Placement errors in the STEP.** Every servo stands 0.5 degrees off square. The drive horn axes read
(0, -0.0087, 1) for the pan and (-1, -0.0087, 0) for the lift. One servo is off by 1 degree: the
wrist flex (-0.9998, 0, -0.0175). Mates in Fusion did not hold the servo models exactly square. The
model keeps the axes as drawn, so its axes are 0.5 to 1 degree from the references'.

**Things left out of the robot** (`LEFT_OUT`):

- **The driver board.** It stands 85 mm below the base's foot and 75 mm behind it, not mounted on
  the arm in this file. Its two connector housings, however, stand where a mounted board would put
  them: 5.6 mm from the base, touching nothing. They are kept, on the base, as nylon.
- **The cables.** They are flexible, and two of them (`Component1` and `Component6`) cross a joint,
  so no rigid link holds them. Their 3.85 cm^3 would be about 8 g at a copper and PVC cable's
  ~2 g/cm^3 (a rough figure). The STEP's "Steel" would make them 30 g.

## Links: one human input, the rest by geometry

`PRINTED` names which printed parts form which link. That table is the one hand-made input, taken
from which meshes each link of the maker's URDF holds. Links and joints take the names of the
maker's URDF: `base_link` ... `moving_jaw_so101_v1_link`, and `shoulder_pan` ... `gripper`. Menagerie
names its bodies without `_link`, but a URDF holds links and joints in one namespace. A link called
`gripper` next to a joint called `gripper` made the URDF writer rename the joint `gripper_2`, so the
comparison could not pair it (see the gaps below).

Every other leaf goes to a link by contact (`lib/step_links.py`):

1. Each printed part is meshed at 0.2 mm (OCCT `BRepMesh`) and sampled at 12 points/mm^2 into one
   scipy KD-tree.
2. Each leaf is sampled at 300 points on its surface. A point within `TOUCH` = 0.5 mm of a link's
   printed part touches that link.
3. The leaf goes to the link it touches most.
4. A servo goes by its three case shells as one unit; the motor, board and gears follow the case.
5. Its horns go to the link they touch most, never the case's link.
6. A leaf that touches nothing goes to the nearest link (only the two connectors, at 5.6 mm).

The result had no ambiguous case:

| link | printed parts | servos (body) | horns | screws/nuts | other | mass (g) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| base_link | Base_SO101, Base_08p, WaveShare plate | servo 1 | - | 6 fillister | 2 connectors | 132.1 |
| shoulder_link | Rotation_Pitch, Motor_holder_Base | servo 2 | servo 1 drive + idler | 4 fillister, 8 M3 | - | 99.9 |
| upper_arm_link | Upper_arm | servo 3 | servo 2 drive + idler | 4 fillister, 8 M3 | - | 106.2 |
| lower_arm_link | Under_arm, Motor_holder_Wrist | servo 4 | servo 3 drive + idler | 4 fillister, 8 M3 | - | 108.4 |
| wrist_link | Wrist_Roll_Pitch | servo 5 | servo 4 drive + idler | 2 fillister, 8 M3 | - | 81.8 |
| gripper_link | Wrist_Roll_Follower | servo 6 | servo 5 drive | 4 fillister, 5 M3, 2 nuts | - | 81.5 |
| moving_jaw_so101_v1_link | Moving_Jaw | - | servo 6 drive + idler | 8 M3 | - | 26.8 |

The contact counts were clear-cut:

- A fastener touched one link, 135-240 of its 300 samples.
- A servo case touched its holder with 36-82 samples per shell, and the next link with 0-7.
- A horn touched the driven link with 39-93 samples.

This reproduces the maker's link grouping exactly, including the idler horns on the driven link.
Assigning a servo leaf by leaf did not work: the motor, board and gears touch no printed part.

**Speed.** Grouping all leaves takes about 4 s. Two things were too slow before that:

- The first attempt used `trimesh.proximity.closest_point` on the printed meshes: 7 GB of memory and
  minutes per screw. The KD-tree of samples replaced it.
- Each `leaf.located(world)` cost 7 s and 400 MB, because build123d's `located` deep-copies the node
  **with its parent**, which is the whole read tree. `Leaf.placed()` deep-copies with the parent
  mapped to None.

## Joints from geometry

Each servo is the joint between the link that holds its case and the link its drive horn touches.
That pair names the joint through `JOINTS`, and an unexpected pair fails the build.

- **The axis** is the drive horn's rim: its largest cylindrical face (r = 9.6 mm), read through
  `BRepAdaptor_Surface(face).Cylinder().Axis()`.
- **Its direction** is chosen to point out of the servo, from the middle case to the horn. A
  positive angle is right-handed about the outward horn normal.

| joint | point (mm) | direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| shoulder_pan | (-0.10, -7.54, 62.43) | (0, -0.0087, 1.000) |
| shoulder_lift | (-13.65, -38.86, 119.76) | (-1.000, -0.0087, -0.0001) |
| elbow_flex | (-12.14, 73.58, 148.47) | (-0.9999, -0.0086, -0.0088) |
| wrist_flex | (-10.06, -60.58, 152.30) | (-0.9998, 0.0002, -0.0175) |
| wrist_roll | (7.06, -118.78, 152.08) | (0, -1.000, -0.0088) |
| gripper | (22.76, -145.70, 172.06) | (1.000, -0.0001, 0.0087) |

**Checks of the axes against the parts' own bores:**

- The follower's r = 12, r = 10 and r = 2.7 bores are coaxial with the wrist roll horn (0.00 mm).
- The moving jaw's two r = 10 pivot bores are coaxial with the gripper horn (0.00 mm).
- The follower's servo-6 screw holes are parallel to the gripper axis and symmetric about it.

So the STEP is self-consistent, and the jaw's pivot is the gripper servo's axis. The mechanism is the
skill's pattern, applied to sub-assemblies:

1. Each link is a `Compound` of its parts, at the identity.
2. A `RevoluteJoint` goes on the parent's compound at the world axis.
3. A `RigidJoint` goes on the child's compound at `Plane(origin, z_dir=axis)`.
4. The pair is connected at 0, so the STEP pose is the as-built state.

## Limits: a self-collision sweep

The STEP has no limits, and the servo has no stop of its own: Waveshare's ST3215 specification says
"Mechanical Limited Angle: No Limit" (360-degree magnetic encoder). `so101_ranges.py` finds each stop
from the exported description:

- It loads the MJCF in MuJoCo.
- It samples every visual mesh except fasteners and connectors at 0.6 mm.
- It marks the still side in a 1 mm voxel grid.
- It turns one joint at a time, 1 degree per step, up to 360 degrees each way, with the other joints
  at the STEP pose.
- A stop is the child link's samples landing in the parent link's voxels more than 40 points over
  their count at zero.

Only the child-against-parent pair counts. A first version counted every pair, and the folded pose
then stopped shoulder_lift at -17 degrees by the forearm hitting the shoulder. That is a
self-collision of the pose, not a joint stop. The run takes 41 s.

| joint | ours (deg) | what stops it | Menagerie (deg) | ours in their coordinate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| shoulder_pan | -115 / +114 | rotation-pitch on servo 1's case / on Base_08p | -110 / 110 | -113.8 / 115.2 |
| shoulder_lift | -192 / +13 | upper arm on the base motor holder, both ways | -100 / 100 | -102.3 / 102.7 |
| elbow_flex | 0 / +191 | wrist motor holder on the upper arm (already touching as built) / under arm on the upper arm | -96.8 / 96.8 | -101.1 / 89.9 |
| wrist_flex | -98 / +100 | wrist roll pitch on the wrist motor holder, both ways | -95 / 95 | -100.3 / 97.7 |
| wrist_roll | -169 / +175 | follower cradle on the wrist roll pitch, both ways | -157.2 / 162.8 | -187 / 157 |
| gripper | -23 / +98 | jaw on the follower's cradle / on servo 6's case | -10 / 100 | -10.4 / 110.6 |

- The total travels agree within 5-10 degrees, except wrist_roll: 344 degrees here against 320.
- The wrist_roll stops are grazing contacts. Changing the sampling seed moved them 3-10 degrees
  (-166, -169, -176), so they are the least certain numbers in the model.
- A 1 mm voxel means about 1 mm of clearance is counted as contact.
- The parts split for collision (below) add a cut face. The first sweep after the split counted it
  as a surface and stopped the gripper at +53. The sweep now drops the triangles that lie on another
  piece of the same part.

## Mass

| category | how | mass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| printed parts (11) | effective density from the maker's print settings (below) | 276.9 g |
| servos (6) | body density calibrated to 55 g per servo, STEP steel horns kept | 325.6 g |
| fasteners | the STEP's steels (7850-8030 kg/m^3) | 33.8 g |
| connectors | nylon (bd_materials) | 0.5 g |
| **total** | | **636.8 g** (Menagerie 645.4 g with its 13.4 g camera mount, 632.0 g without; the maker's URDF 632.0 g) |

**Printed parts.** The maker's README ("Printing the Parts") gives PLA+, a 0.4 mm nozzle at 0.2 mm
layers, and **15 % infill**. PLA is 1240 kg/m^3 (bd_materials' PLA; NatureWorks Ingeo 1.24 g/cm^3).
Each part's effective density is a solid skin 0.8 mm thick under its whole surface (two 0.4 mm
perimeters, and four 0.2 mm solid layers top and bottom: the slicer defaults for a 0.4 mm nozzle),
with 15 % of the material in the rest:

```
skin = min(1, area * 0.8 mm / volume)
rho  = 1240 * (skin + 0.15 * (1 - skin))
```

`lib/step_links.printed_density` computes it from the part's own B-rep volume and area. It ranges
from 387 kg/m^3 (the upper arm, skin fraction 0.19) to 822 kg/m^3 (the base motor holder, 0.60).
Printed solid, the same parts would weigh 709 g instead of 277 g, and the arm about 1.07 kg.

**Servos.** The STEP's servo is a simplified model with overlapping solids. Its leaves hold 41.3 cm^3,
while the whole case measures 45.2 x 24.7 x 35 mm, which is 39 cm^3 (Waveshare's specification). At
the STEP's PPS density each servo would weigh 78 g. The ST3215's published mass is used instead:
**55 g**. The body leaves get one density, 1081 kg/m^3, which makes a two-horn servo weigh 55 g with
the STEP's steel horns (10.4 g) kept at 7850 kg/m^3. The wrist-roll servo has no idler horn in the
STEP, so it weighs 50.6 g.

The 55 g is the commonly published Feetech STS3215 / Waveshare ST3215 figure. From this sandbox, the
Waveshare wiki page (fetched) carries no weight, and the vendor pages answered 403, so **the 55 g is
quoted, not re-read**. With the STEP's own PPS, the arm would weigh 774 g.

**Against the references, link by link** (`reports/so101.md`):

| link | ours (g) | Menagerie (g) | maker's URDF (g) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| base_link | 132.1 | 147.0 | 147.0 |
| shoulder_link | 99.9 | 100.0 | 100.0 |
| upper_arm_link | 106.2 | 103.0 | 103.0 |
| lower_arm_link | 108.4 | 104.0 | 104.0 |
| wrist_link | 81.8 | 79.0 | 79.0 |
| gripper_link | 81.5 | 87.0 (+13.4 camera mount) | 87.0 |
| moving_jaw | 26.8 | 12.0 | 12.0 |

- **The jaw** is +123 %, for a physical reason. Its link here carries servo 6's two steel horns
  (10.4 g) and 8 M3 screws, which turn with it. The references leave the horns on the servo, in the
  gripper link.
- **The base** is 15 g light. The cause was not traced. The references' base may hold parts this
  model leaves out (the board, the cables), or their base parts may be printed denser.
- **Camera mount.** The STEP has none, so Menagerie's camera mount (13.4 g) has no counterpart. That
  difference is expected.
- **Inertias.** Principal moments run 35-70 % above the references' for shoulder through wrist. Our
  links carry the servo as 55 g of distributed solids and the printed parts as shells. The references
  come from Onshape at their own densities.
- **Total gravity torque** error is 5.8 % RMS against Menagerie and 1.5 % against the maker's URDF.

## Collision

Collision is a simulation's choice, so it is made in the MuJoCo function (`src/so101_mujoco.py`), on
the spec, by each part's label path (`cad.mujoco.parts` lists the 133):

| part | in the MuJoCo function | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| printed parts | left as the mesh the spec gives every part (MuJoCo hulls it) | the parts that bear contact; one hull each is the cheapest shape that follows them |
| static jaw (follower) and moving jaw | split in the model into `_cradle` and `_finger`, two parts, each left as its mesh | one hull of the follower would fill the space between the jaws (cradle top to finger tip) and make grasping impossible |
| servo case shells (3 per servo) | `fit(s, part, "box")` | the case is a box; three boxes follow its stepped outline |
| servo motor, board, gears | its collision geoms deleted (`s.delete(geom)` over `collisions(s, part)`) | inside the case |
| horns, screws, nuts, connectors | deleted the same way | inside or flush with the parts they hold |

The result is 31 collision geoms (18 box, 13 mesh), against Menagerie's 30 (20 box, 6 sphere, 3 mesh,
1 capsule).

**The jaw split.** Each jaw is cut by `bd.split` with the plane through the gripper servo case's
farthest point along the jaw:

- for the follower, along the wrist roll axis;
- for the moving jaw, along the jaw's direction square to its pivot.

Each piece keeps the part's effective density, so the mass is unchanged.

**Contacts at rest.** Validation warns of 20 geom pairs that intersect at qpos0, all
base_link-to-shoulder_link: the rotation-pitch hull covers the base servo's top. MuJoCo's
`filterparent` does not apply here. The base hangs from the world by a fixed joint, so MuJoCo welds it
into the world body, and a body whose parent is the world is never filtered. This needed
`<contact><exclude body1="base_link" body2="shoulder_link"/>`, which the MJCF writer now writes for
every jointed pair touching at rest (gap 3): the saved file validates with no warning.

## Actuators and the commanded pose

In the MuJoCo function, none of it from the references:

- One position actuator a joint, named for it. Its force bound is the model's: `limits(arm, joint,
  effort=2.94, velocity=4.71)`, the ST3215's stall torque and no-load speed at 12 V, which the spec
  carries as each joint's `actfrcrange` (and the URDF as `<limit effort velocity>`).
- `kp` 60 N*m/rad with `dampratio` 1, chosen, not measured: the arm stretched level sags under a degree.
- No joint armature, damping or dry friction: nobody has measured them on this servo.
- A key `home` that commands shoulder_pan 30, shoulder_lift -60, elbow_flex 60, wrist_flex 30,
  wrist_roll 45 and gripper 30 degrees, as `qpos` and as `ctrl`.

`python src/check.py so101` is the proof it works: started at `home`, after 2500 steps of 2 ms (5 s)
under gravity the worst joint (elbow_flex) is 0.0072 rad from its command, with no contact.

## Frames and states

- **`tcp`** is a site on the follower's finger piece: the centroid of the static jaw's last 8 mm along
  the wrist roll axis, at (7.0, -224.0, 140.5) mm. Its Z points out of the jaw (the wrist roll
  direction) and its X along the jaw pivot.
- **No `home` key.** No physical source defines one: the maker's calibration pose is a software
  convention, and this STEP's pose is the rest pose.
- **No mechanism state** for the same reason.

  bodies.
- `cad urdf validate`: OK. The root is `base_link`, with 8 links, 7 joints (6 movable) and a total
  mass of 0.6368 kg.
- In MuJoCo 4.0 the model has 7 bodies, 6 hinges, 164 geoms, 27 meshes and 1 site (`tcp`).
- **Snapshots** (`out/so101/`), taken in-process under Xvfb (gap 4):

`Moving_Jaw_SO101 v3` and `Wrist_Roll_Pitch_SO100 v15`. The most likely cause is **a different
revision of the wrist and jaw parts**, not an error in either.

## Gaps in text-to-cad

1. **Vendor STEP densities read 1000x too light.**
   - Fusion declares `SI_UNIT(.KILO.,.GRAM.)` over `METRE^3`, for example
     `MEASURE_REPRESENTATION_ITEM('density measure', POSITIVE_RATIO_MEASURE(1060.), #158863)`.
   - The read gave `material.material.density == 1.06` for ABS and 7.85 for steel.
   - This was found here and reported. It is fixed on the branch (`document.correct_step_densities`,
     commit f7c60a5); the model has no workaround.
2. **URDF names.** Fixed: the URDF writer names links and joints each in its own kind, and says a rename.
   Links and joints shared one namespace in the URDF writer (documented in
   `export/urdf.py`). The joint `gripper` became `gripper_2`, silently, because a link was named
   `gripper`.
   - Menagerie's own names (body `gripper`, joint `gripper`) cannot survive a URDF export.
   - Reproduction: a two-link model whose child link and joint share a label, exported to `.urdf`.
   - The writer should warn when it renames a joint, since poses and couplings name joints.
   - Worked around by naming the links as the maker's URDF does (`*_link`).
3. **No contact exclusion.** Fixed: the MJCF writer excludes every jointed pair touching at rest; any
   other pair is the MuJoCo function's (`s.add_exclude(bodyname1=, bodyname2=)`). A mechanism whose ground link is fixed to the world has its first joint's
   parent welded to the world body. MuJoCo's `filterparent` then does not filter that pair, so
   concave parts nested at the first joint collide at rest.
   - The MJCF writer could emit `<contact><exclude>` for every pair joint's two bodies (at least
     where the parent is the ground). Alternatively, a declaration could say it.
   - Reproduction: any model whose child link's hull overlaps its ground parent's hull.
4. **A snapshot of the exported robot under `xvfb-run` fails** with "no X display for OpenGL" when the daemon was
   started without a display. The CLI renders in the daemon, which never sees the DISPLAY that
   `xvfb-run` gives the CLI, and the error's own hint (run under Xvfb) does not help.
   - Worked around with `cad.engine.api.snapshot(src, out, pose=..., daemon=False)` under `xvfb-run`.
5. **`located` copies the whole read tree.** `step.read` returns a tree whose leaves keep their
   parent, and build123d's `located` and `moved` deep-copy a node with its parent: the whole assembly.
   - Outside the engine this cost 7 s and 400 MB per call (and BRep copies), and an exploration
     script reached 8.8 GB.
   - Inside the engine `_sharing` keeps the B-rep shared, but every call still copies every Python
     node of the tree.
   - A reader that wants leaves placed alone needs `copy.deepcopy(leaf, {id(leaf.parent): None})`.
     `Leaf.placed()` does this. A `step.read` helper that hands back flattened, placed leaves would
     avoid it.
6. **No convex decomposition.** Fixed: `cad.mujoco.decompose(s, part)`, in the MuJoCo function, collides
   a part as CoACD's convex hulls of its mesh and keeps the visual whole. A concave printed part collided as one hull.
   Splitting it in the model (`bd.split`) works, but a split part's two meshes meet on the cut, which a
   mesh-based check then counts as surface (handled in the sweep). The model here still splits its
   jaws; decomposing them in the MuJoCo function instead is the follow-up.
7. **No joint limits from geometry.** Nothing in text-to-cad finds a joint's stops. The sweep here is
   about 150 lines over the exported MJCF. A `ranges` command (or a posed `overlap_volume` sweep on
   the document) would make it standard.
8. **Nested STEP bodies lose their material.** Fusion bodies nested in a component
   (`Upper_arm_SO101 v9:1/SOLID`) read with no material, while top-level bodies read "ABS Plastic".
   Whether the file assigns them one was not checked. The model sets every printed part's material
   itself, so this did not matter here.

**The samples' tooling, not text-to-cad.** `fetch.py` narrowed Menagerie's sparse checkout to the robot
fetched last. After a `fetch.py wxai`, `menagerie/robotstudio_so101` was gone, and `compare.py so101`
could not load it. It was restored with `git -C menagerie sparse-checkout add robotstudio_so101`.

## What worked, what did not
